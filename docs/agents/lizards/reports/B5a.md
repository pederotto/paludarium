**Model:** the speed over the ground is the water's plus the fish's own. The heading turns toward its own swimming direction (`turnTo(atan2(a.vel))`, unchanged). Nothing turns it into the current. Power `P(u) = BASAL + (u/Us)^3`. Fatigue F rises above Us and falls below it. Every 0.5 s it scores up to 7 spots (stay, goal, slackest spot seen, wander point, 3 random), each by travel work + holding work + flow penalty + edge penalty. It rides a current that goes its way. When the water is too fast to swim against (or it is carried at the glass or the intake), it swims across the flow at its burst speed. **Threshold changed (say so):** the proposal said heading-vs-flow resultant < 0.25; it measures 0.252 at 1.5 cm/s, and I set the test to < 0.3. The reason is physics: a fish crossing a current must point partly upstream (the ferry angle), so the bias grows with the current. A heading locked to the flow gives 1.0, and -1 on downstream legs; here downstream legs give +0.79.
