# Premade sets: how they are made, checked and what is in them

Written 7 Oct 2026 after run "sets" (audit and repair of the premade terrariums, new species and plants, two showcase sets). The sets are the ready-made
terrariums the generator builds (`src/sim/generator.js`, recipes in `src/content/presets.js`). Blackboard of the run: `paludarium master/.agents/sets/`
(BRIEF, BOARD, reports S1/S2/S3, before/after pictures); this page is the repo's copy of what lasts.

## 1. A set is a recipe over a layout

`PRESETS[id]` (presets.js) names a real place and the plants and animals it has. The generator builds a **layout** (`BUILDERS.<layout>`: cascade, suriname, blackwater, stream, jar, karst, swamp, canyon, highland...), then
**drops everything that is not on the set's lists** (`Gen.allowPlant/allowAnimal`: no stand-ins), then adds the set's own `flora` and `stock`.
Recipe fields: `biotope`, `place`, `featured`, `ref` (reference tank), `water` (share of the floor under water, a promise the layout now keeps), `climate`, `plants`, `animals`, `swap`, `flora` [[id, n, zone]], `stock` [[id, n per standard tank, zone]], `env`, `needs`, `blockedBy`.
Knobs the layouts read (run "sets", S1): `level` (water height as a share of the tank's), `pool` (size of the pool or lagoon), `rate` (pump flow), `bed` (sand, soil = mud, gravel), `rocks` (river stones on the bed), `sparse` (fewer logs), `mix` (logs/roots/boulders), `wall` (back-wall style, moss, rock, relief: `rockWall`), `clump` (plant a kind in groups), `gear` and `env` (a chiller and a cooling set point for a cool place). Unset knobs build exactly what they built before (same seeds).
The generator tops a species up to its stock count and starts the featured animal in the front-middle of the default view.

## 2. The audit (what was wrong, what was done)

`node tools/set-audit.mjs [--counts=<file>]` cross-checks every set against the species sheets (`SPECIES` temp, humidity, pH, GH, flow, flock, territory, `eats`), the set's own climate, its biotope lists, the tank room (`sim/scale.js`) and a coarse region table; severity wrong / thin / ok. `tools/steps/set-counts.mjs` (browser) writes what a build really holds after settle. `tests/setaudit.test.mjs` keeps the rules: no "wrong" finding, no predator beside its prey, 4 or more plant kinds, flora/stock inside the set's own lists, cool places carry a chiller, the promised water share is real.
At the start (91eb9a0): 5 wrong, 28 thin, 0 ok of 33. The causes, by sets hit:
- every set sat under the plants-per-m2 bar (35, a guess taken from the sets that looked right); the allow-list dropped what the layout placed;
- 13 sets shared one lagoon layout and held about 25 % of the tank as water whatever `water` said (stream 5.8 L in a 240 L tank, aripo 6.1 L); the layouts now honour the recipe (stream 42.5 L, aripo 15.1 L, araguaia and atlantic 60 L, chinampa 102 L);
- cool places ran warm (chinampa 23.0 C, oakpond 22.7, beechseep 22.7, stream 22.7): the recipes carry the existing chiller (17.5 / 18.3 / 16.9 C measured after settle);
- real wrong pairs: toad + shrimp (stream: the shrimp left, a pale chub came in), panther + snail (matano), 6 pygmy sunfish for one territory, 1 red-eyed tree frog and 1 marbled newt (groups of 3 and 2);
- 7 sets listed javamoss and no builder placed it;
- trims: `tideline` (hidden, empty, needs mangrove and brackish water) is deleted, `fernjar` is merged into `jar` (four crew species, every species still featured).
The 13 lagoon sets now differ by wall style, bed, hardscape mix, stones, planting and flow (thumbnail contact sheets: `BB/shots/S1/before` and `after2`); they still share one silhouette: a real layout per place is future work.
Comfort run (`tools/steps/gen-check.mjs`, 5 game days, 2 seeds): 14 of 62 builds lost display animals at the start (shanpond pearl danios "current too strong", beechseep and bocasslope "air too dry", oakpond marbled newts, matano shrimp cold at 26 C). Fixed by set recipes (sponge filter for still sets, chiller + fogger, 28 C for Matano). Left, engine side: strawberry frogs, bumblebee toads, reed frogs and geckos starve in 5 UNFED days (a keeper feeds them; the owner, 7 Oct: most likely the hunting logic, which another session is fixing, so these are not set faults: re-run `gen-check` on bocasslope, sierrapools, reedpool and karst once it lands); the isopod's 18-28 C range is too narrow for the cool sets.

## 3. New species and plants (S2)

All are **procedural**: SDF fish and crustacean bodies (`render/creatures/bodies/streamfish.js`, `crustaceans.js`, `small.js`) and `Builder` ribbon plants (`sim/plants.js`), written from general knowledge (every number tagged known or guess in `BB/reports/S2.data.md`). No scan, no skeleton, no muscles, no painted skin: they are level with the game's other procedural fish, not with the scanned animals (Species board: about 37 % of its stages against the fire salamander's 80 %; plants about 51 % against the remade orchids' 93 %).
Animals: `tanichthys` White Cloud Mountain minnow, `zacco` pale chub, `hillloach` hillstream loach, `bullhead` European bullhead, `bedotia` Madagascar rainbowfish, `matanoshrimp` Matano shrimp, `tylomelania` Matano rabbit snail, `cambarellus` Mexican dwarf crayfish. Plants: `nidus` bird's-nest fern, `crypt` Cryptocoryne, `hartstongue`, `miscanthus` silvergrass, `heliconia`, `aponogeton`, `pandanus`, `limnobium`, `sago`, `tussock`, `crowfoot`.
Touch points of a new animal: SPECIES row, body, HABITAT row, economy row, Field guide text (`species-info.js`), portrait (`tools/bake-portraits.mjs --only=<id>`), editor batch, biotope list, tests (`tests/s2-species.test.mjs`, `caresheet`, `habitat`). A plant: PLANTS entry + SPREAD row, `plant-info.js`, economy row, portrait, biotope list.
`sim.js` careStress got one clause, `flowMin` (a species that needs a current: hillloach 0.5, zacco 0.3, bullhead 0.2; the last two are guesses). Not done: the hillloach does not cling to rock, the bullhead does not hold a cave, the dwarf crayfish claws do not animate (movers belong to the animals/obstacles work); frame cost not measured (`crowfoot` is the heaviest plant, 7.1k vertices).

## 4. Showcase sets (S3)

Both are new layouts in `generator.js` (`BUILDERS.canyon`, `BUILDERS.highland`) with their recipes and biotopes in `src/content/presets-showcase.js` (spread into `PRESETS`, `PRESET_ORDER` and `BIOTOPES` by two glue lines in presets.js and biotopes.js). `hydro.js` and `terrain.js` are untouched: the engine simulates only water above the main pool, so a river is a sloped bed that ends in the pump pool, and rapids, riffles and eddies are bed shape (`tests/showcase.test.mjs`, `tools/steps/showcase-flow.mjs`).
**Canyon: Liwu River, Taroko Gorge, Taiwan** (tiers `long`, `show`; rebuilt on 7 Oct after the owner's picture: a rock gorge with a waterfall, a shelf stream and a deep lagoon; the first version ran a river along the front glass and is gone). Two massifs of layered rock (the left to 80 %, the right to 90 % of the height: terraced by `ledged`, gullies down the faces, a `blocks` back wall), a valley between them. A **wall fall** out of the back wall (`outlets: [{ x, y, wall: true }]`, from about 80 % of the height) lands in a basin on a rock **shelf** (one short rise above the lagoon), a stream runs across the shelf and **falls over its lip into the lagoon**, a deep clear pool that fills the front (level 0.48 of the height, the pump reservoir; the front sill keeps the glass profile low, so there is no brown slab). The lagoon floor is gravel and cobbles with a branch, a root tangle and cobble groups; ledges carry ferns, hart's-tongue, bird's-nest fern, silvergrass, begonia and creeping fig, the lagoon Cryptocoryne and java fern. Stock unchanged: pale chub (6 on `long`, 12 on `show`), up to 3 hillstream loaches, wild-type shrimp (14) in the lagoon, isopods and springtails on the ledges. Recipe knobs: `level` 0.48, `rate` 320, `canister`, `caps`, `hillDepth`, `wall`, `crestL`/`crestR` (all read by `BUILDERS.canyon`; `GEN_KNOBS='{"canyon":{...}}'` in `tools/steps/gen-check.mjs` tries a change without editing a file) and the air pump (gear `airpump`, `env.air` 0.8). `tools/steps/canyon-look.mjs` takes the six views (default, fall, each massif, lagoon floor, three-quarter, top).
**Measured (7 Oct, seeds 1-3, 3 game days):** 6 of 6 `gen-check` builds clean (no deaths, no NaN, oxygen 7.5 mg/L on `long` and 8.9-9.0 on `show`); `long` 73.1-73.5 L and `show` 296-299 L (targets 80 and 300; the first canyon held 85 and 329 with a deep front pool and a brown slab), pump 283 / 272 L/h, 6-10 falls reported (the wall fall, the shelf pools and the lip falls), builds in 0.9-1.5 s; default view and `?webgl` (WebGL 2) render on both tanks with no console errors (the first `?webgl` `show` frame caught the loading screen, so only `long` is a picture check there).
**Still short:** the stream across the shelf is 35-45 cm long, not the tank's full length (the old front river ran 47 % of the width and cost a 17 cm brown slab); the wall fall is a thin ribbon (about 2 cm); the rock palette is the game's grey (`MAT.rock`/`stone`), not the reference's warm reddish sandstone; overhangs are not possible in a heightfield; the lagoon looks dark at the front glass (the water's own fog and light); frame cost of the denser planting (138-155 plants on `show`, 97 before), the Windows laptop and the iPhone were not measured; tiers `wide`, `grand`, `tower` were not run, so they are not offered. The animals were left as they were (the owner put them aside on 7 Oct).
**Air pump (new equipment, owner's word 7 Oct):** `GEAR.airpump` (Water group, level 2, 35), a Care panel slider (`E.air`, 0 to 1), a controller actuator (`air`) and an oxygen sensor (`SENSORS.oxygen`), a running cost (`upkeep.js`, sized by litres), saved with the env (`Env.KEYS`, snapshot), and `AIR_O2` = 2.2 mg/l at full setting in the main pool's oxygen target (a third of it for the other bodies; a guess, like the filter and fall terms beside it). `tests/airpump.test.mjs`. Not done: a visible bubble column (an emitter in `render/jet.js` registered at the pool's centre but nothing showed at the default camera or at 26 cm, so it was taken out: the jet renderer's plunge-plume bubbles are the place to look), a placeable airstone, a flow effect from the stirring. Checked headless on WebGPU: the pump at full lifts the canyon's pool oxygen, no console errors.
**Highland: upland brooks of the Teutoburg Forest and Harz, Germany** (tiers `wide`, `standard`; three seeds stable, 3-day gen-check 4/4). A plateau at 66 % of the height, three seeps that merge into one brook, two terraces with falls into a stony pool; cooled to 15.5 C (chiller, fan, fogger). Stock: fire salamanders (2-5, on the banks), bullheads (1-2, in the pool), springtails; no shrimp (the bullhead eats them) and no isopods (too cold). Water is 5-9 L: it is a terraces-and-streams set, not a water-capacity set; fire salamander larvae in the brook are not modelled. `beechseep` (the old thin seep) stays, now cooled and planted.
Why these two places: the canyon takes the game's wild-type shrimp and a rapids fish for Taiwan's gorges (Taiwan's own cyprinids are not in the game, so the pale chub stands for them); the highland uses the best-built animal in the game (the rebuilt fire salamander) and the plants the Teutoburg biotope already listed. Other places would also fit (an Andean cloud-forest highland for the orchids needs frogs the game does not have).

## 5. Textured skins (what is real, what is still flat)

The owner asked for "colored AND properly textured skin, no plasticky skin with an approximate colour path". Three pilots show the way; the rest is not done.
- **Hillstream loach (done as a pilot; REPLACED on 9 Oct by the owner's own Meshy model, see "Baked Meshy models" below; the kept input is `art-src/creatures-sets/_hillloach_procedural.glb`):** `tools/bake-hillloach-skin.mjs` (plain Node) meshes the SDF body, gives it body-space UVs (u along the length, v around the section, seam on the belly, fins as flat sheets in their own atlas rectangles) and bakes a 1024 px atlas: colour, tangent-space normal and ORM (R occlusion, G roughness, B wetness). The colour atlas holds the owner's top, side and belly views resampled onto the UVs (the maze pattern reads crisply at 5 cm); relief and ORM are authored and carry no colour. Imported with `npm run import-creatures -- --src=art-src/creatures-sets` (`REAL_CM` and `BUDGET_ID` entries), 428 kB + 156 kB, about 17 MB of GPU memory for the three maps with mipmaps; draw calls not measured. Still wrong: the pectoral and pelvic fins are stretched crops with washed patches (they need their own UV islands and outlines), the dorsal fin seen from above is baked into the back, the flank relief is weak.
- **Five plants (done as pilots):** hart's-tongue fern, bird's-nest fern, Cryptocoryne, spongeplant and lace plant carry the orchids' leaf-map path (`plantMaterial` `leafMap`, `leafHue`, `leafRelief`, `wax`; loader `src/sim/plant-leaves.js`; maps in `public/assets/plants/<id>-{leaf,relief,tint}.webp`, 128 x 512 each; bake `art-src/plants/leafbake.py`, `hartstongue_bake.py`). The tint atlas holds resampled reference data, the outline, veins and relief are authored; they stay separable so a procedural tint can replace it. Seen in the game on WebGPU, console clean. Still plastic: regular painted veins, the same strap shape on every frond, the lace plant's lattice drawn at one scale.
- **Not done:** the other four fish (their bakes were running when the owner stopped the agents: `tools/bake-colormap.mjs` / `colormaps.js` per-vertex colour maps are in the tree as the fallback and for portraits only), the invertebrates (matano shrimp pilot unfinished, crayfish, rabbit snail), six plants (screw pine, sago, Heliconia, silvergrass, bunchgrass, crowfoot).
- **Lesson from the guppy session (7 Oct):** building a fish body from scratch (an SDF loft, then a scripted Blender anatomy) failed twice; the owner's verdict was "terrible, i'll provide glb". For anything the owner judges by eye, ask for their own model at the base-fit gate (their image-to-3D GLBs, as for the toad, skink, gecko and guppy), then prep (orient, real size, weld, drop debris and the buried inner shell, decimate fins apart from the body, label fins by connected sheet) and paint on the model's own UVs: `art-src/guppy/prep_glb.py` and `render/creatures/guppypaint.js` on branch `feat/guppy`. Entry point for the owner's future GLBs of the new fish (from the guppy session, 7 Oct): `blender -b --factory-startup -P art-src/guppy/prep_glb.py -- <raw.glb> <outdir> <name> --sl=<standard length cm> --headAxis=<±x|±y>` is generic for any fish (orient, real size, remove the buried inner shell, fins by lateral line-span plus connected sheets, separate fin and body decimation to 12k/4k, coords and parts maps baked into the model's UVs, eye centre); `tools/guppy-import.mjs` is guppy-specific (copy its pattern: dedup only accessors, meshes and textures, or the body and fin materials merge), then `npm run import-creatures -- --baked=<id>`; `guppypaint.js` is guppy-specific (a fixed species can keep the owner's texture). Write-up: the rig playbook's "From the guppy" section and `.agents/guppy/STATE.md`. The SDF fish of this run are placeholders in that sense.

**Meshy models (owner's word, 7 Oct evening, "i have a meshy subscription now"):** the owner generates a body per species in Meshy from the reference pictures and sends the GLB through the Drop. Route: `node tools/meshy-prep.mjs <in.glb> <id> --cm=<real length> --head=<-x|+x|+z|-z> [--fold=<cm>:<share>]` (turns the head to +Z, scales to the real length, centres, bakes it into the vertices; `--fold` pulls spike fins toward the body), id and real length into `REAL_CM` in `tools/import-creatures.mjs`, `npm run import-creatures -- --src=art-src/creatures-sets` (then rewrite `manifest.json` with the compact skeleton format as the baked path does: the plain path expands every skeleton into 7,000 lines), original to `art-src/raw/`, `node tools/bake-portraits.mjs --only=<id>`, a close-up with `tools/steps/s2-closeup.mjs` (`ANIMAL=<id>`). Done for the European bullhead (9.8k triangles, 10.8 cm) and the Madagascar rainbowfish (5.1k, 9.6 cm): real textured skins (colour, normal and metallic-roughness maps), unlike the procedural bodies they replace. A Meshy file named `..._generate.glb` is geometry only (no UVs, no texture): it needs Meshy's Texture step (`..._texture.glb`) before it can be used. The Blender connector's own `generate_3d` was tried and refused: no generator is switched on there. The prepared copies in `art-src/creatures-sets/` (9 MB each) are not committed; the originals are in `art-src/raw/` and the prepared ones come back with `node tools/meshy-prep.mjs art-src/raw/meshy_bullhead_texture.glb bullhead --cm=10.8 --head=-x` and `node tools/meshy-prep.mjs art-src/raw/meshy_bedotia_texture.glb bedotia --cm=9.6 --head=-x --fold=1.0:0.3`. Generated bodies are not scans: proportions are not yet measured against the targets, fins are single sheets (not marked as membranes), no skeleton or muscles.

**Texturing geometry-only Meshy files from the owner's photos (owner, 8 Oct: "texture them yourself"):** a `..._generate.glb` has no UVs, so `art-src/meshy/photo_prep.py <photo> <base> [--mode=diff|sat|both --thr= --erode= --dorsal=]` finds the animal in the photo (background fitted as a smooth surface; `sat` or a high `--thr` when it casts a shadow), bleeds its edge colours outward from a few pixels inside the outline (blurred with distance: the back and belly read these rows from the side projection) and writes the texture, a normal map made from the photo's fine detail and the per-column outline; `blender -b --factory-startup -P art-src/meshy/project_texture.py -- <in.glb> <base> <out.glb> --axis=x|y` finds the head end by correlating the model's height profile with the photo's (printed: 0.88 against 0.44 for the chub, 0.89 against 0.10 for the minnow; 0.60 against 0.53 for the shrimp, check by eye), turns the head to -X and projects the photo from the side, each vertex keeping its share of the length and of the body's height at that place, so the model's outline lands on the photo's. Both flanks read the same side. Result: real scales, eye, gill cover and fin rays on the pale chub and the White Cloud minnow (in the game, portraits re-baked); a red spotted shrimp and an orange crayfish were textured assets only at first (the game animates crustaceans from a baked leg rig, so a static body would glide); since 9 Oct they are rigged and in the game ("Baked Meshy models" below). Weak spots: the belly and the top are smooth (edge rows of the photo), fins are single sheets, the pectorals stand out (folded 40 % toward the body for the chub), proportions not measured against the targets.

**Fin membranes (owner, 8 Oct: "do it"):** the game shades any model part whose material name contains "fin" as a membrane (see-through, lit from behind, passive flutter: `render/creatures/glb.js` `matIdFor`, `material.js`, `instanced.js` isFin; `finish.finOpacity` says how dense). `art-src/meshy/mark_fins.py <in.glb> <out.glb>` splits a fish model's faces into `skin` and `fin` materials sharing one texture: a face is a fin when it has no body behind it (a ray along and against its normal, followed through every layer, finds no far side: single sheets and thin double layers), small patches are turned over (welded by position, texture seams split vertices), `--debug=1` tints the fins magenta. `art-src/meshy/build_fish.sh` runs the whole route for the four fish (photo, projection, fins, prep); `art-src/creatures-sets/overrides.json` carries `finOpacity` (0.6 for the chub, minnow and rainbowfish, 0.8 for the bullhead). The importer's `dedup` now keeps uniquely named materials, otherwise it merged `fin` back into `skin` when the two were alike (the Meshy-textured files). Seen in game from the side: translucent ray-veined dorsal, anal, caudal and pelvic fins. Not caught: the bullhead's big fleshy pectoral fans (too thick for the test, they stay opaque) and the rainbowfish's wire-thin pectorals. No fin movement of their own beyond the shader's passive flutter, no fin rays as bones.

**Baked Meshy models (owner, 9 Oct: the loach "is already textured so use that", "shrimps and crayfish like the crab session did"):** three models go through `node tools/bake-creature.mjs <id>` instead of the importer, as the panther crab does, because a Meshy texture is a patchwork of tiny charts that no decimation keeps (the importer's mesh reduction smeared the loach's maze pattern into streaks). Each is welded, decimated (or, for the 1.1k-triangle crayfish, subdivided once and smoothed: `subdiv`), rigged, unwrapped afresh (xatlas, 1024 px) and painted by `tools/rig/colorsrc.mjs` from the owner's texture at the nearest point of the original surface; the baked colour map matches the original's average colour to one level (loach 133/124/97 against 132/123/97) and its spread of light and dark. Rigs by the model's own pieces (`tools/rig/pieces.mjs`: each leg, claw, swimmeret strip and antenna is a separate connected piece of mesh):
- **Hillstream loach** (`hillloach`, 14.0k triangles, 6.5 cm, 695 kB + 244 kB): the owner's "Maze Patterned Pleco" file (the Meshy name; it is the loach, the head toward +z). `rig: 'fish'` (`tools/rig/fish.mjs`) carries no legs but marks the fins' membrane vertices (material id 2, from `art-src/meshy/mark_fins.py`'s marked copy, `art-src/meshy/work/hillloach_fin.glb`; fins 2,101 of 6,995 vertices), `centerY` puts its origin at the middle of its body. Finish in `tools/paint/eyes.mjs` (`finOpacity 0.8`). The analytic eyes of the old body are gone; the eye is in the texture.
- **Matano shrimp** (`matanoshrimp`, 11.7k triangles, 2.4 cm rostrum to telson, 366 kB + 72 kB): `tools/rig/meshyshrimp.mjs`, the dwarf shrimp's rig ids (pincers 15 / 16, walking legs 1 to 4, antennae 7 / 8, swimmerets 10, eggs 14, tail flick pivot, analytic black eyes): 8 legs, 2 swimmeret strips, 8 antennae, 2 eyes, 22 eggs found. The body piece is cut into whips, tail fan and eyes by `segment` run on it alone (thin 0.02). Colours from the photographic projection (`art-src/raw/meshy_matanoshrimp_textured_by_projection.glb`, whose faces Blender turned inside out: the bake takes the geometry from the generated file and only the colour from the textured one, `colorPre` turns the frames).
- **Mexican dwarf crayfish** (`cambarellus`, 4.5k triangles, 2.3 cm carapace to telson, 199 kB + 50 kB): `tools/rig/meshycray.mjs`, shrimp rig ids with the two claws as 15 / 16 and the crab's pinch and feeding cycle on them (`finish.claws` fitted by `tools/rig/claws.mjs` with `clawIds`; `render/creatures/instanced.js` `pinchFeed`, shared by the crabs' 5 / 6 and the crayfish's 15 / 16, the dwarf shrimp's own pincer dip is used only when there is no `claws` fit). The model's tail fan stands on end (its plates 50 degrees from the horizontal, "upside-down" to the owner's eye): the bake turns it flat behind the abdomen's tip by 45 degrees after the colour is painted (`post`, `postBaked`). Portraits are framed at the species scale now (`engine/portraits.js`: a scaled species had its claws cut off).
Check with `node tools/bench.mjs <id> --src=glb --lod=hi --views=side,top,three,low` (without `--src=glb` the bench draws the procedural body), the feeding cycle with `--pose=feed`, and in a tank with `tools/steps/crust-look.mjs` (`PRESET=matano IDS=matanoshrimp`, `PRESET=chinampa IDS=cambarellus`). The bench holds the rig inputs still, so walking is not visible in its strips (the old dwarf shrimp shows the same). Not checked: the shrimp's and crayfish's frame cost (a school of 24 shrimp at 11.7k triangles each), the tail flick and the swimmerets in a real swim, the crayfish's claws pinching another animal, Windows laptop, iPhone, `?webgl`.

## 6b. Scanned landscape, plants and ground (8 Oct 2026)
- **Models.** 56 generated (Meshy) landscape, wood and plant scans from the owner became 11 hardscape types (`PIECES` in `sim/decor.js`: mesa, pillar, arch, limestone, scatter, bigrock, fallenlog, sculpt, hollowlog, logpile, branch) and 10 model plants (`PLANTS` in `sim/plants.js`). Both are `lazy`: they load in the background after the title screen and a game waits for them (`Game.buildTank`). The meshes are low-poly with a colour (and normal) map BAKED from the full scan (`art-src/meshy/bake_lowpoly.py`): simplifying a scan with its own UVs gives stripes, see CREDITS.md and `tools/meshy-decor.mjs` / `meshy-piece.mjs`.
- **Ground by climate** (`content/ground.js`): forest, temperate, tropical, river, blackwater, limestone, arid palettes swap the pictures behind the terrain slots (`World.setGround`, saved with the tank); a set's biotope picks its palette (`BIOTOPE_GROUND`). The baked textures: `tools/bake-ground.py`, `public/assets/ground/`.
- **Hardscape looks** (`HARDSCAPE` in `content/presets.js`, `Gen.remap`): a recipe says whether its boulders become granite, river or limestone stone and its logs fallen, sculpted or hollow wood: cascade, stream, highland, blackwater and the three new sets.
- **New sets:** `satoyama` (Honshu pond: lotus, lilies, hosta, royal fern, trillium, fire-bellied toad, pale chub), `choco` (Colombian Pacific slope: parrot heliconia, forest ginger, crimson moon fern, green and black poison frog), `orinoco` (river bend: sunken wood, cardinals, corydoras; the maze-patterned Meshy fish turned out to be the owner's hillstream loach, not a pleco, and is the `hillloach` species). Species still to come in them: Japanese tree frog (the harlequin poison frog is in `choco`).
- **Plants keep clear of each other:** a scanned plant has a `room` (the share of the two crowns' reach kept free), floating pads never overlap (`Plants.crowdingAt`).

## 6c. The land/water rule of the land frogs (owner, 8 Oct 2026)
Every frog and toad has `land` (the share of the floor that is dry land it wants) and `landTol` 0.1 (`sim/animals.js`; the dart frogs, which had none, 0.8). The share is measured on the USABLE floor (`World.usable`): a cell a piece of hardscape stands on, or a plant covers, is HALF DEAD space (it counts 0.5 toward the land or the water it sits on), share = usable land / (usable land + usable water). Outside the band: a stress of 0.05 a day with the reason and the numbers (`Sim.tankRules`, `habitat.js landWhy`), and the Animals tool refuses to release the frog (`Animals.placement`). The generator stocks through the same refusal, so a set whose water is wrong has no frogs: `tools/steps/land-rule.mjs` measures every land-frog set, `land-tune.mjs` finds the `level` and `pool` knobs that put a set mid-band.
